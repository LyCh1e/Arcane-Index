# IT Ethics

Domain overview for ethics and professional conduct in computing — covering ethical theory, behavioural ethics, privacy law, intellectual property, information security ethics, and AI ethics.

## What Is This Domain?

IT ethics applies philosophical ethical frameworks to the decisions and dilemmas specific to computing professionals. It asks: *How should IT professionals ensure that technology brings benefits and does not harm users, clients, or society?*

This domain sits at the intersection of philosophy, law, social science, and technology practice.

## The Five Workable Ethical Theories

Quick reference for applying ethical reasoning:

| Theory | Core Test | Best For |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Kantianism** | Can I universalise this maxim? Am I treating people as ends, not merely means? | Individual rights, dignity, duty |
| **Act Utilitarianism** | Does this action maximise total happiness for all affected? | Policy decisions, resource allocation |
| **Rule Utilitarianism** | Would a rule permitting this, if universally followed, maximise happiness? | Establishing general standards |
| **Social Contract** | Would rational people agree to this from behind the veil of ignorance? | Fairness, just institutions |
| **Virtue Ethics** | What would a person of good character do in this situation? | Professional conduct, character development |

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| Categorical Imperative | Kant's two-formulation absolute moral rule: universalisability + humanity as end |
| Principle of Utility | Action is right to the extent it increases total happiness of affected parties |
| Veil of Ignorance | Rawls' device for impartial reasoning: choose principles without knowing your social position |
| Cognitive Dissonance | Discomfort from contradictory beliefs; leads to rationalisation |
| Moral Licensing | Feeling licensed to act unethically after previous ethical behaviour |
| Confirmation Bias | Tendency to seek information confirming existing beliefs |
| Conformity Bias | Tendency to align behaviour with group norms rather than independent judgement |
| Conflict of Interest | Private interests that could improperly influence professional judgement |
| Whistleblowing | Reporting illegal or unethical organisational activity to authorities |
| CSR | Corporate Social Responsibility — organisational ethical accountability |
| PDPA | Singapore's Personal Data Protection Act |
| PII | Personally Identifiable Information |
| Hacker Ethic | Belief system valuing open information, sharing, and decentralisation |
| Fair Use | Limited use of copyrighted material without permission for education, criticism, research |
| Trade Secret | Confidential proprietary information giving competitive advantage |
| Explainability | Ability to explain AI decision-making in human-understandable terms |
| Digital Divide | Gap between those with and without effective access to digital technology |
| Deepfake | AI-generated synthetic media used to impersonate real people |
