# Agile Project Management

**Source**: The 2020 Scrum Guide — Ken Schwaber & Jeff Sutherland; PSM I study materials.

---

## What I Know

Agile project management is an iterative, empirical approach to delivering value under uncertainty. The Scrum framework is the primary lens here: it applies the three pillars of empiricism — transparency, inspection, and adaptation — within fixed-length Sprints to help teams build the right thing incrementally.

The PSM I certification track is currently active, using the 2020 Scrum Guide as the canonical reference.

## Key Ideas

- **Scrum is purposefully incomplete** — it defines the minimum structure required; teams fill in the tactics
- **Sprint = timebox, not a project phase** — a fixed event of ≤ one month; the next Sprint begins the moment the previous one ends, with no gap
- **Cross-functional team = self-sufficient team** — the Scrum Team collectively holds all skills needed to deliver a "Done" Increment without external dependencies
- **Product Goal = long-horizon commitment** — the Product Backlog exists to achieve the Product Goal; a new Goal is not set until the current one is met or abandoned
- **Definition of Done is non-negotiable** — if a Backlog Item does not meet the DoD it cannot be included in the Increment; undone work returns to the Product Backlog
- **Sprint Planning answers three questions**: (1) Why is this Sprint valuable? → Sprint Goal; (2) What can be Done? → Sprint Backlog selection; (3) How will the chosen work be done? → plan
- **Sprint Goal is a commitment, not a wish** — the Developers may negotiate scope with the Product Owner, but the Sprint Goal is fixed for the duration of the Sprint
