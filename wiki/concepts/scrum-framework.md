# Scrum Framework

Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organisations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems. It is founded on empiricism — knowledge comes from experience and observation — and lean thinking, which reduces waste and focuses on essentials.

Scrum is purposefully incomplete. It defines only the parts required to implement Scrum theory; tactics and specific practices are left to the teams using it.

---

## Scrum Theory

### Foundation

Scrum combines two philosophical underpinnings:

| Underpinning | Principle |
|---|---|
| **Empiricism** | Knowledge comes from experience; decisions are based on what is observed |
| **Lean thinking** | Reduce waste; focus on essentials |

Scrum uses an **iterative, incremental** approach to optimise predictability and control risk.

### Three Pillars

All Scrum events and practices implement these three empirical pillars:

#### Transparency
The emergent process and work must be visible to those performing it and those receiving it. Important decisions are based on the perceived state of the three formal artifacts. Low-transparency artifacts lead to decisions that diminish value and increase risk.

> Transparency enables inspection. Inspection without transparency is misleading and wasteful.

#### Inspection
Scrum artifacts and progress toward agreed goals must be inspected frequently and diligently to detect undesirable variances or problems. Scrum's five events provide the cadence for this inspection.

> Inspection enables adaptation. Inspection without adaptation is pointless. Scrum events are designed to provoke change.

#### Adaptation
If any aspect of a process deviates outside acceptable limits, or the resulting product is unacceptable, an adjustment must be made **as soon as possible** to minimise further deviation.

Adaptation is impaired when the people involved are not empowered or self-managing. A Scrum Team is expected to adapt the moment it learns anything new.

## Scrum Values

Five values give direction to the Scrum Team regarding work, actions, and behaviour:

| Value | Expression |
|---|---|
| **Commitment** | The team commits to achieving its goals and supporting each other |
| **Focus** | Primary focus is on the Sprint's work to make the best possible progress |
| **Openness** | The team and stakeholders are open about work and challenges |
| **Respect** | Members respect each other as capable, independent professionals |
| **Courage** | Members have the courage to do the right thing and tackle tough problems |

When these values are embodied, the empirical pillars of transparency, inspection, and adaptation come to life — building trust.

## Scrum Team

The fundamental unit of Scrum is a small team: **one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers**. There are no sub-teams or hierarchies — it is a cohesive unit focused on one objective at a time: the **Product Goal**.

**Key characteristics:**
- **Cross-functional** — members collectively have all skills necessary to create value each Sprint
- **Self-managing** — the team internally decides who does what, when, and how
- **Small** — typically 10 or fewer people; smaller teams communicate better and are more productive
- If a team becomes too large, it should reorganise into multiple cohesive Scrum Teams sharing the same Product Goal, Product Backlog, and Product Owner

The entire Scrum Team is accountable for creating a **valuable, usable Increment every Sprint**.

---

### Developers

Developers are committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint. Required skills are broad and domain-dependent. Developers are always accountable for:

1. Creating a plan for the Sprint — the **Sprint Backlog**
2. Instilling quality by adhering to the **Definition of Done**
3. Adapting their plan each day toward the **Sprint Goal**
4. Holding each other accountable as professionals

---

### Product Owner

The Product Owner (one person, not a committee) is accountable for **maximising the value of the product** resulting from the Scrum Team's work. This includes effective **Product Backlog management**:

- Developing and explicitly communicating the **Product Goal**
- Creating and clearly communicating Product Backlog items
- **Ordering** Product Backlog items
- Ensuring the Product Backlog is transparent, visible, and understood

The Product Owner may delegate these activities but remains accountable. For the Product Owner to succeed, the entire organisation must respect their decisions — visible in the ordering of the Product Backlog and the inspectable Increment at Sprint Review.

Those wishing to change the Product Backlog can do so only by **convincing the Product Owner**.

---

### Scrum Master

The Scrum Master is accountable for **establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide** and for the **Scrum Team's effectiveness**. They are true leaders who serve the Scrum Team and the larger organisation — not managers.

**Serving the Scrum Team:**
- Coaching team members in self-management and cross-functionality
- Helping the team focus on high-value Increments that meet the Definition of Done
- Removing impediments to the team's progress
- Ensuring all Scrum events take place and are positive, productive, and within the timebox

**Serving the Product Owner:**
- Helping find techniques for effective Product Goal definition and backlog management
- Helping the team understand the need for clear and concise backlog items
- Helping establish empirical product planning for a complex environment
- Facilitating stakeholder collaboration

**Serving the organisation:**
- Leading, training, and coaching the organisation in its Scrum adoption
- Planning and advising Scrum implementations
- Helping employees and stakeholders enact an empirical approach for complex work
- Removing barriers between stakeholders and Scrum Teams

## Scrum Events

The **Sprint** is the container event for all other events. Each event is a formal opportunity to inspect and adapt Scrum artifacts. Failure to operate events as prescribed results in lost opportunities for inspection and adaptation. Optimally, all events are held at the same time and place to reduce complexity.

---

### The Sprint

Sprints are the **heartbeat of Scrum**, where ideas are turned into value.

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | Fixed length, one month or less |
| Cadence | A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous one concludes |
| Contains | Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective |

**During a Sprint:**
- No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal
- Quality does not decrease
- The Product Backlog is refined as needed
- Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner as more is learned

Shorter Sprints generate more learning cycles and limit risk to a smaller time frame. In complex environments, only what has already happened may be used for forward-looking decision-making — burn-downs and cumulative flow charts are useful but do not replace empiricism.

A Sprint may be **cancelled** only by the Product Owner, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

### Invalid Sprint Types

The following are **not valid Scrum constructs** — they do not exist in the framework and indicate a misapplication of Scrum:

| Invalid Type | Why It Violates Scrum |
|---|---|
| **Hardening / Stabilisation Sprint** | Quality is built in every Sprint via the DoD; deferring quality to a separate Sprint defeats empiricism |
| **Sprint 0** | The first Sprint must produce a usable Increment — there is no pre-Sprint warm-up |
| **Release Sprint** | Increments may be released at any time; releasing is not a Sprint-end gate |
| **Design Sprint** | Design is an ongoing activity within every Sprint, not a dedicated phase |

---

### Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning initiates the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed. It is created by the **collaborative work of the entire Scrum Team**.

**Three topics:**

| Topic | Question | Who leads |
|---|---|---|
| **Why** | Why is this Sprint valuable? | Product Owner proposes; whole team defines Sprint Goal |
| **What** | What can be Done this Sprint? | Developers select from Product Backlog via discussion with PO |
| **How** | How will the chosen work get done? | Developers decompose items into tasks of one day or less |

The Sprint Goal must be finalised prior to the end of Sprint Planning.

The **Sprint Backlog** = Sprint Goal (why) + selected Product Backlog items (what) + plan for delivery (how).

**Timebox:** maximum **8 hours** for a one-month Sprint; shorter for shorter Sprints.

---

### Daily Scrum

The Daily Scrum inspects progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapts the Sprint Backlog as necessary.

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | 15 minutes |
| Frequency | Every working day of the Sprint |
| Participants | Developers (PO and SM attend only if actively working Sprint Backlog items) |
| Location | Same time and place every day |

Developers choose whatever structure they want, as long as the Daily Scrum focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produces an actionable plan for the next day.

**Role clarity:**
- The Scrum Master **ensures the Daily Scrum occurs** — they do **not** conduct or run it
- The Product Owner **does not attend** — unless actively working on Sprint Backlog items, in which case they participate *as a Developer*
- The Daily Scrum is **not** a technical problem-solving meeting — problem-solving happens separately afterwards

The Daily Scrum is not the only time Developers may adjust their plan — they meet throughout the day for more detailed discussions. It improves communication, identifies impediments, and promotes quick decision-making.

---

### Sprint Review

The Sprint Review inspects the **outcome of the Sprint** and determines future adaptations.

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Timebox | Maximum **4 hours** for a one-month Sprint |
| Participants | Scrum Team + key stakeholders |
| Output | Adjusted Product Backlog; collaborative decisions on what to do next |

The Scrum Team presents the results of their work. The team and stakeholders review what was accomplished and what has changed in the environment. The Sprint Review is a **working session**, not a presentation.

> The Sprint Review should never be considered a gate to releasing value — an Increment may be delivered to stakeholders at any point during the Sprint.

---

### Sprint Retrospective

The Sprint Retrospective plans ways to **increase quality and effectiveness**. It concludes the Sprint.

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Timebox | Maximum **3 hours** for a one-month Sprint |
| Participants | Scrum Team |
| Scope | Individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and Definition of Done |

The team inspects how the last Sprint went, identifies what went well, what problems were encountered, and how those problems were (or were not) resolved. Assumptions that led the team astray are identified and their origins explored.

The team identifies the most helpful changes to improve effectiveness. The most impactful improvements are addressed as soon as possible — they may even be added to the next Sprint Backlog.

## Scrum Artifacts and Commitments

Each artifact contains a **commitment** that enhances transparency and focus, and provides a basis against which progress can be measured.

| Artifact | Commitment |
|---|---|
| Product Backlog | Product Goal |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal |
| Increment | Definition of Done |

---

### Product Backlog

The Product Backlog is an **emergent, ordered list** of what is needed to improve the product — the single source of work for the Scrum Team.

- Items that can be Done within one Sprint are deemed **ready for selection** in Sprint Planning (typically achieved through refinement)
- **Product Backlog refinement** is the ongoing act of breaking down items into smaller, more precise pieces, adding details such as description, order, and size
- Developers are responsible for **sizing**; the Product Owner may influence by helping them understand trade-offs

#### Commitment: Product Goal

The Product Goal describes a **future state of the product** that serves as a target for the Scrum Team to plan against. It is the long-term objective — the team must fulfil (or abandon) one Product Goal before taking on the next.

The rest of the Product Backlog emerges to define *what* will fulfil the Product Goal.

---

### Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog is composed of:
1. The **Sprint Goal** (why)
2. The set of **Product Backlog items** selected for the Sprint (what)
3. An actionable **plan for delivering the Increment** (how)

It is a plan **by and for the Developers** — a highly visible, real-time picture of the work planned for the Sprint. It is updated throughout the Sprint as more is learned, with enough detail to inspect progress in the Daily Scrum.

#### Commitment: Sprint Goal

The Sprint Goal is the **single objective for the Sprint** — a commitment by the Developers that provides flexibility in the exact work needed to achieve it. It creates coherence and focus.

If work turns out differently than expected, Developers collaborate with the Product Owner to negotiate the scope of the Sprint Backlog **without affecting the Sprint Goal**.

---

### Increment

An Increment is a **concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal**. It must be:
- **Additive** to all prior Increments
- **Thoroughly verified** — all Increments work together
- **Usable** — to provide value

Multiple Increments may be created within a Sprint. An Increment may be delivered to stakeholders **prior** to the end of the Sprint.

Work cannot be considered part of an Increment unless it meets the **Definition of Done**.

#### Commitment: Definition of Done

The Definition of Done is a **formal description of the state of the Increment** when it meets the quality measures required for the product. The moment a Product Backlog item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born.

- If a Product Backlog item does not meet the Definition of Done, it **cannot be released or presented** at the Sprint Review — it returns to the Product Backlog
- If the Definition of Done is an organisational standard, all Scrum Teams must follow it as a minimum
- If not, the Scrum Team must create one appropriate for the product
- Multiple Scrum Teams working on the same product must share the **same Definition of Done**

## Quick Reference

### Event Timeboxes (one-month Sprint)

| Event | Timebox |
|---|---|
| Sprint | ≤ 1 month |
| Sprint Planning | ≤ 8 hours |
| Daily Scrum | 15 minutes |
| Sprint Review | ≤ 4 hours |
| Sprint Retrospective | ≤ 3 hours |

### Accountabilities at a Glance

| Role | Primary Accountability |
|---|---|
| Developers | Creating a usable Increment; Sprint Backlog; Definition of Done adherence |
| Product Owner | Maximising product value; Product Backlog management; Product Goal |
| Scrum Master | Establishing Scrum; team effectiveness; removing impediments |

### Artifact–Commitment Mapping

| Artifact | Commitment | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| Product Backlog | Product Goal | What is the long-term objective? |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal | Why is this Sprint valuable? |
| Increment | Definition of Done | What does "Done" mean? |

## Key Principles to Remember

1. **Scrum is immutable** — implementing only parts of Scrum does not produce Scrum
2. **The Sprint Goal is fixed; scope is flexible** — Developers negotiate scope with the PO without changing the Goal
3. **Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint**
4. **The Product Owner is one person, not a committee**
5. **Releasing need not wait for Sprint Review** — Increments may be released at any time
6. **50% of effort should be self-managing** — Scrum Teams decide internally who does what, when, and how
7. **The Sprint Retrospective closes the Sprint**, not the Sprint Review

## Scaled Scrum

When multiple Scrum Teams work on the **same product**:

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Product Owner | **One** per Product Goal / Product Backlog — shared across all teams |
| Product Backlog | **One** — single source of truth regardless of team count |
| Increment | **One integrated Increment** — all teams contribute to a single combined Increment |
| Synchronisation | Teams **need not synchronise** with each other — coordination happens through the shared backlog and events |
| Definition of Done | **Shared minimum** — all teams must comply; individual teams may make their DoD stricter but not weaker |

> Multiple Scrum Teams working on the same product must share the same Product Owner, the same Product Backlog, and the same (or stricter) Definition of Done.
