# Scrum Master Exam — Study Notes (80 Questions)

Based on the 80-question CSM/PSM/ACP preparation video. Organised by theme. Cross-reference [[learning/scrum-open-assessment-study-notes]] for the 30-question Scrum Open bank; this page focuses on new and deeper nuances surfaced by the larger question set.

Reference: [[scrum-framework]] for full framework notes.

---

## Waterfall vs. Scrum

| Aspect | Waterfall | Scrum |
|---|---|---|
| Activity duration | Estimated/calculated up front using today's knowledge | Time-boxed |
| Early customer feedback | **Rarely offered** — no mechanism for it | Built in via Sprint Review |
| Risk accumulation | Accumulates over long cycles | Controlled per Sprint |
| Planning | Detailed up front | Just enough, evolving |

**Factors that increase complexity** (all three apply):
- Larger number of people on the project
- Longer duration of the project
- Batching a large number of features into one big release

**Complex adaptive problems** (e.g. software):
- Are **stochastic** — hard to predict even using past history
- Cannot be resolved by deterministic planning alone

**How Scrum limits the risk of "unknowingly doing something wrong":**
> Short iterations (Sprints) — near the end of each Sprint, stakeholder feedback identifies whether work was worthwhile. The risk of pursuing a wrong direction is **limited to the cost of one Sprint**.

## Empiricism — Precision

**Empiricism does NOT eliminate uncertainty.** It *reduces* risk by providing frequent feedback and course-correction points where more information may be available.

What empiricism provides:
- Frequent opportunities to view different **possibilities**
- Frequent opportunities to make **informed decisions**, thus reducing risk

> **Transparency** in empiricism means: the significant aspects of the process must be defined by **common standards** and made **visible** so that observers share the same understanding. It is not about "clear thinking" or "morality".

**Three pillars** belong to **empirical process control theory** (not lean, not PDCA, not Six Sigma):
> Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation

---

## Formal Inspection and Adaptation Opportunities

> **Q: What are the formal opportunities for inspection and adaptation?**
> **A: None of the listed options (Sprint, Scrum Team, Product Increment) — the answer is the four events:**

The four events used for inspection and adaptation:
1. Sprint Planning
2. Daily Scrum
3. Sprint Review
4. Sprint Retrospective

The Sprint itself is a **container** for these events, not an inspection event.

Not having any of these events results in **reduced transparency** and lost opportunity to inspect and adapt.

## Scrum Framework Composition

> **Scrum framework consists of:** Scrum Teams and their associated **roles, events, artifacts, and rules**.

- "Scrum standards" is not the term used (even though Definition of Done is a standard)
- "Product development processes" are not a part of the framework itself

---

## Scrum Is Immutable

> **Scrum is immutable. Yes.** Implementing only parts of Scrum is not Scrum.

Three things Scrum is *not* (from 30-question bank, reinforced here):
1. A process that eliminates complexity (it provides a framework for *dealing with* complexity)
2. A pick-and-choose methodology
3. Traditional processes + self-organisation replacing Project Managers

## Roles — Deeper Nuances

### Servant Leadership

> **"Servant leader" is NOT a new name for "Project Manager."** False.

The Scrum Master is a servant leader, but this is a **fundamentally different approach** — not a re-labelling. It is not the traditional command-and-control style associated with project management.

| Traditional | Scrum |
|---|---|
| Project Manager | Scrum Master (servant-leader) |
| Command and Control | Self-management |

### Scrum Master Is Not the Team Manager — But *Is* a Management Position

> The Scrum Master manages the **Scrum framework implementation**, not the people. The role is a management position because of this. A coaching style of servant leadership is required.

- The Scrum Master manages Scrum deployment and coaches the team on Scrum
- A person with strong PM experience delivering results is **not** the right fit (servant-leadership + Scrum experience is what is needed)

### Product Owner — Continuous Collaboration

> The Product Owner **continuously collaborates** with the developers, sometimes almost daily. They do not hand over the backlog and leave, nor do they freeze the backlog.

- Product Backlog evolves based on frequent new insights
- PO works with the team to communicate changing needs and clarify ongoing work questions
- PO is accountable for backlog **transparency, visibility, and understanding**

### Management's Role

> Management has **no official role or accountability** on a Scrum Team.

However, organisational/senior management support **is needed** (a critical success factor):
- Support the **Product Owner** with insights and information on high-value capabilities
- Support the **Scrum Master** to encourage organisational change that fosters empiricism, self-management, and bottom-up intelligence

### Who Communicates With Stakeholders

> A customer with relevant product information should talk to the **Product Owner**.

However: **only** the Product Owner communicates with stakeholders is **False** — the entire Scrum Team collaborates with stakeholders, not just the PO.

### Scrum Master and Product Understanding

> If Developers do not have enough understanding of the product they are developing, the Scrum Master **must care** — coaching Developers to create high-value products includes ensuring that goals, scope, and product domain are understood by everyone.

### SM's Role With Artifacts

> The Scrum Master **coaches the team to increase transparency** of artifacts — they do not own, decide the format of, or maintain the artifacts themselves.

## Self-Managing Teams

**What self-managing teams can manage:**
- Their work toward a common goal

**What they cannot self-manage:**
- Human resource matters (leave, firing, dress code, office matters)
- Requirements outside the Product Backlog — no ad hoc high-priority requests from executives

**Accountability is collective:**
> Each team member works on individual tasks, but the **entire team is accountable** for overall progress toward the common goal. No individual "owns" a Product Backlog item's value.

**Sprint Backlog items are collectively owned:**
> Even if an individual works on a specific item, the team collectively takes ownership. The Scrum Master should coach the team toward collective ownership, not individual assignment of "ownership".

---

## Product Backlog — Key Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who can suggest items? | **Anyone** — PO gets input from any stakeholder or team member, but has final say on content and order |
| Ordering | Decided by PO — whatever is deemed most appropriate |
| Can it change mid-Sprint? | **Yes** — can be updated any time by the PO |
| How detailed at start? | Only the initially known and best understood requirements |
| How much for the first Sprint? | **Just enough items** for the first Sprint + staffed Scrum Team is sufficient |

---

## Backlog Refinement Sessions

> **There is no technical development activity during backlog refinement.**

- No coding, no testing
- Purpose: breaking down and further defining items; adding details, order, and size

---

## Sprint Backlog — Key Rules

- Belongs **solely to the Developers** — if a new task is identified during the Sprint, the **Developers** add it (not PO, not SM)
- Modified **throughout** the Sprint as more is learned
- Sprint Backlog items selected into the Sprint are **not frozen** — scope may be negotiated with PO without affecting the Sprint Goal
- The Sprint Goal is **fixed**; scope is **flexible**

**Sprint Backlog and retrospective improvement items:**
> The Scrum Guide states improvement items *may* be added to the Sprint Backlog for the next Sprint. The blanket statement "Sprint Backlogs *contain* items for improvement from retrospectives" is **False** because it is not a rule — it is optional.

---

## Definition of Done — Key Rules

**What the DoD is used for:**
> To identify unfinished work — it spells out all that is required to complete the work and is used by the team to assess what is yet to be done.

**How the team defines the DoD:**
> Neither "always creates a new one" nor "checks with PO if required" is correct. The rule is:
- If an **organisational standard** exists → use it as a minimum
- If **no standard** → Scrum Team creates one appropriate for the product

**Cross-functionality and the DoD:**
> A Scrum Team that hands the increment to an *external* team for testing to meet the DoD is **not** truly cross-functional. A cross-functional team must possess **all** the competencies needed to deliver a valuable increment that meets the DoD without external help.

**DoD vs. acceptance criteria:**

| | Definition of Done | Acceptance Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Quality checklist for a usable Increment | Specification of expected business features |
| Scope | Applies to all increments | Applies to individual backlog items |

---

## Progress Tracking

> **Velocity is NOT a good metric** for tracking the progress of product development.

The real mark of progress in Scrum is the **delivery of a usable product Increment**.

**Burn-down / burn-up charts:**
- Useful tools — but **do not replace the importance of empiricism**
- In complex environments, only what has already happened may be used for forward-looking decisions

**Product Backlog item value:**
> The value attached to a Product Backlog item is **an estimate** — it is based on assumptions and must be validated by releasing the item. Scrum facilitates early validation by making it available as a usable Increment.

**Reading a burn-down chart:**
- **Upward spike** mid-Sprint → Developers **added new work** to the Sprint Backlog
- Product burn-down: Sprints on x-axis; Sprint burn-down: days of the Sprint on x-axis

## Scrum Events — Deeper Nuances

### Sprint Start

> A new Sprint starts **immediately after the Retrospective event of the current Sprint** concludes. The Retrospective is the **last** event; Sprint Planning is the **first** event of the new Sprint.

### Sprint Planning Going Over Timebox

Root causes (select all that apply):
- **B: The team did not invest enough in backlog refinement** — insufficient refinement forces planning to do what refinement should have done
- **D: Developers are trying to get a perfect and detailed Sprint plan** — Sprint Planning only needs enough planning to get started

Not a cause: "The Scrum Master does not moderate and control participants" — moderation is not the SM's primary tool here.

### Daily Scrum Reduced to Once a Week

> The Scrum Master should **coach the team on the essentials of conducting Daily Scrums** — this is not acceptable. Scrum is immutable; the Daily Scrum is a prescribed event.

### Inspections — Who Performs Them

In Scrum events, inspections are performed by those doing the work and those with product knowledge:
- **Product Owner** ✓
- **Developers** ✓
- **Invited stakeholders** ✓ (at Sprint Review)

**Not** performed by: corporate audit groups, senior management, technical/domain experts (unless invited as stakeholders).

---

## Sprint Review — Deeper Nuances

**Who must participate:** The **Scrum Team** always participates. Stakeholders participate only if invited by the Product Owner.

**Purpose of presenting the Increment:**
> To **elicit feedback** and foster collaboration — **not** to get sign-off, provide a status report, or mark completion.

**Sprint Review requires:**
- Active participation of **invited stakeholders**
- **Inspection and adaptation** activities

**Does not require:**
- Product Owner sign-off
- Transition sign-off

### End of Product

> After a Sprint Review where the PO decides development is over:
- The **next step is usually a Retrospective** (last event of a Sprint)
- **However**, if the PO decides development is truly complete, there is **no need** for a Retrospective
- Transition documentation is defined as part of the **Definition of Done** — a done Increment already has it created

---

## Sprint Retrospective vs. Sprint Review

| | Sprint Retrospective | Sprint Review |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | **Team's processes and people** | **The product** (Increment) |
| When | Last event of the Sprint | Second to last event |
| Participants | Scrum Team only | Scrum Team + invited stakeholders |

---

## Estimation

> There is **no mandatory estimation unit** in Scrum. Story points, ideal hours, function points, t-shirt sizes — any useful sizing technique may be used. The Developers choose.

---

## Testing Strategy

> There is **no prescribed testing sequence** in Scrum. "Unit → Integration → UAT" is one approach, but it is **up to the team** to find the best testing approach. The requirement is that each Increment is thoroughly tested, all Increments work together, and the Increment meets the Definition of Done.

---

## Scrum Values in Practice

> A Developer who sets aside Sprint work to help an important stakeholder with a task **outside the Sprint Goal** has violated the **Scrum value of Focus**.

The value of Focus means prioritising and completing Sprint work to achieve the Sprint Goal, avoiding unrelated activities during the Sprint.
