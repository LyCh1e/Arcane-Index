## Common Traps and Misconceptions

These are the questions most likely to catch you out — either because the wrong answer sounds plausible, or because the correct rule is counter-intuitive.

---

**Daily Scrum timebox — is it 15 minutes or does it scale with Sprint length?**

> Always **15 minutes**, regardless of Sprint length. The timebox does not shrink for shorter Sprints.

*Trap:* Option "15 minutes for a 4-week Sprint; shorter for shorter Sprints" sounds reasonable but is incorrect. The Daily Scrum is uniquely fixed in this way.

---

**Does the Increment have to be released at the end of each Sprint?**

> **No.** The Increment must be *usable*, but it does not have to be released. It may be released at any time during or after the Sprint — the Sprint Review is not a release gate.

---

**Is Scrum a methodology?**

> **No.** Scrum is a **lightweight framework** for complex problems. It is not a complete methodology, a defined predictive process, or a cookbook of best practices.

*Trap:* "Framework for creating complex products in complex environments" is correct; "A complete methodology that defines how to develop software" is not.

---

**What are three things Scrum is NOT?**
1. Scrum is *not* like traditional processes with self-organisation replacing Project Managers
2. Scrum does *not* eliminate complexity — it offers a framework for *dealing with* complexity
3. Scrum is *not* a pick-and-choose methodology — it is immutable; partial Scrum is not Scrum

---

**Does the Sprint Backlog change during the Sprint?**

> **Yes.** The Sprint Backlog changes and may grow as work emerges. Developers modify it throughout the Sprint. It is *not* fully formulated at Sprint Planning and then frozen.

*Trap:* "The Sprint Backlog is fully formulated in Sprint Planning and does not change during the Sprint" — **FALSE**.

---

**Can the CEO (or anyone external) add an item to a running Sprint?**

> **No one external to the Scrum Team can force changes on the Developers.** If a "very important" item arrives mid-Sprint, the Developers discuss it with the rest of the Scrum Team and decide what to do — they are not obligated to add it.

*Key rule:* No changes that endanger the Sprint Goal. The team decides.

---

**When can a Sprint be cancelled, and who can cancel it?**

> Only the **Product Owner** has the authority to cancel a Sprint, and only when the **Sprint Goal becomes obsolete**. "Not everything will finish" or "the work is too hard" are not valid reasons.

---

**Is there a "Project Manager" role in Scrum?**

> **No.** The Scrum Team consists of Developers, Product Owner, and Scrum Master only. There is no Project Manager role.

---

**How many Product Backlogs exist when multiple Scrum Teams work on one product?**

> **One Product Backlog per product**, regardless of how many teams are working on it. Multiple backlogs make it difficult for Developers to determine what to work on.

---

**Who must share the same Definition of Done when multiple teams work on one product?**

> All Scrum Teams working on a single product must **mutually define and comply with the same Definition of Done**.

---

**What does "timeboxed" mean?**

> A timebox sets a **maximum duration** — the event cannot exceed it. It does not mean the event must take that long, must start at a fixed time, or must have a minimum duration.

---

**When does the next Sprint begin?**

> **Immediately after the conclusion of the previous Sprint.** There is no gap — not "next Monday", not "when the PO is ready".

---

**What should the Scrum Team do during the first Sprint?**

> Create a **valuable and useful Increment** — same as every other Sprint. The first Sprint is not for complete architecture planning, building the entire Product Backlog, or developing a release plan.

---

**Who is accountable for the value of a specific Product Backlog item once selected for a Sprint?**

> **No single Developer.** The **entire Scrum Team is accountable** for creating value every Sprint. Accountability is collective, not individual.

---

**What happens to Scrum if you rename its terms to fit existing organisational terminology?**

> The organisation may not understand what has changed, and the benefits of Scrum may be lost. Scrum's vocabulary is deliberate — it signals a real change in thinking and behaviour.

## Roles

### Product Owner

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Primary responsibility | Maximising the value of the product / work of the Scrum Team |
| Who manages the Product Backlog? | Product Owner |
| How is the Product Backlog ordered? | **However the Product Owner deems most appropriate** — not by size, risk, or value alone |
| Who knows most about progress toward a business objective? | The Product Owner |
| Can the PO be a committee? | **No** — one person only |
| Who can cancel a Sprint? | **Only the Product Owner** |
| Can others change the Product Backlog? | Only by **convincing the Product Owner** |

### Scrum Master

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Primary accountability | Establishing Scrum; Scrum Team effectiveness |
| Management of Developer work | **Not the SM** — the Developers manage their own work |

### Developers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who manages Sprint progress? | **The Developers** |
| Who attends the Daily Scrum (required)? | **The Developers** |
| How much work must they complete per Sprint? | Enough for the Increment to meet the **Definition of Done** |
| Is a Developer replaced freely? | As needed, but account for **short-term reduction in productivity** |

### Management

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Official role in Scrum? | **None** — management has no official role or accountability on a Scrum Team |
| Appropriate contribution | Support the PO with product insights; support the SM to encourage organisational change |

## Events

### Which events are timeboxed?

All five Scrum events have time constraints. Sprint Planning, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective have explicit maximums:

| Event | Timebox (1-month Sprint) | Scales with Sprint length? |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint | ≤ 1 month | Fixed length chosen by team |
| Sprint Planning | ≤ 8 hours | Yes — shorter for shorter Sprints |
| Daily Scrum | 15 minutes | **No** — always 15 minutes |
| Sprint Review | ≤ 4 hours | Yes — shorter for shorter Sprints |
| Sprint Retrospective | ≤ 3 hours | Yes — shorter for shorter Sprints |

*Note: The question "Which events are timeboxed?" (Q8) lists Sprint Planning, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective as the three answer choices — the Sprint and Daily Scrum are not among the options.*

### Daily Scrum

- **Required attendees:** the Developers only
- The PO and SM attend only if they are actively working on Sprint Backlog items (in which case they participate *as Developers*)
- The Developers choose their own format — no prescribed three questions
- **Not** the only time Developers may discuss or adapt their plan

### Sprint Review

- The Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the **outcome** of the Sprint and determine **what to do next**
- It is a **working session**, not a demo or presentation
- **Not** a release gate — Increments may be released before Sprint Review

### Sprint Retrospective

- The Sprint Retrospective **closes** the Sprint (not the Sprint Review)
- Inspects individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done

## Artifacts and Commitments

| Artifact | Commitment | Key Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Product Backlog | Product Goal | One per product; ordered by PO; emergent |
| Sprint Backlog | Sprint Goal | Sprint Goal fixed; scope negotiable; Sprint Backlog evolves |
| Increment | Definition of Done | Must be usable; need not be released |

### Definition of Done

- If an organisational standard exists → all Scrum Teams follow it as a **minimum**
- If no standard → the **Scrum Team creates** one appropriate for the product
- Multiple teams on same product → **single shared DoD**
- Who creates it → **Scrum Team** (not PO alone, not SM alone)
