# FactSet (2018): Golden and death crosses

Source is more than 3 years old: treat as potentially stale.

- **Sample:** US companies with market cap of at least 300 million dollars at the time of the cross; crosses from 31 December 1998 to 31 March 2018 (two recessions, two bull markets); returns tracked at one month, six months and one year, by sector. The number of crosses is not stated.
- **Golden crosses:** "show positive returns over the short term for most sectors"; the expectation of positive performance "holds mostly true" over the longer period.
- **Death crosses:** also positive, and "even outperform golden crosses in our current bull market"; described as an indication "to buy at a low price" rather than a sell or short signal.
- **Sector notes:** energy shows almost no correlation between crosses and price performance; in IT during the bull market, death-cross returns beat golden-cross returns.
- **Conclusion:** "The jury is still out"; golden crosses "still appear to pick winners over the long run, but death crosses appear to pick winners in bull markets".

## Reliability notes

Specific return figures are in charts that were not readable. The cross is defined generally as a short-term over a long-term moving average; the lookback lengths are not given in the text read.
