# The 2020 Scrum Guide

The Scrum Guide is the definitive reference for the Scrum framework, authored by its co-creators Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland. First published in 2010 and updated periodically, the November 2020 edition is the current canonical version. Scrum originated in the early 1990s from work presented at the OOPSLA Conference in 1995.

## Purpose

The Guide defines Scrum precisely. Each element serves a specific purpose; removing or modifying elements limits the benefits of Scrum or renders it useless. The framework is intentionally incomplete — it defines only what is required to implement Scrum theory, leaving tactics and practices to the teams using it.

## Scope of This Ingest

The full 2020 Scrum Guide, covering:
- Scrum Theory (empiricism, lean thinking, three pillars)
- Scrum Values
- Scrum Team accountabilities (Developers, Product Owner, Scrum Master)
- Five Scrum Events with timeboxes
- Three Scrum Artifacts with their Commitments

## Key Facts

- **Creators**: Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland
- **First formal definition**: OOPSLA Conference, 1995
- **Team size**: typically 10 or fewer people
- **Sprint length**: one month or less, fixed
- **Three roles**: Developers, Product Owner (one person), Scrum Master
- **Five events**: Sprint (container), Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective
- **Three artifacts**: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment
- **Three commitments**: Product Goal, Sprint Goal, Definition of Done
