# Recent Context (Finance)

## Last Updated

2026-11-24 (autoresearch). **Session: EMAs in trading, and what the 9, 20 and 200 show.** New: [[synthesis/research-emas-in-trading]], [[concepts/exponential-moving-average]], [[concepts/ema-9-20-200-roles]], [[concepts/moving-average-trading-evidence]] and seven source pages.

**EMA = k x price + (1 - k) x previous EMA, with k = 2 / (N + 1)** (0.20 for the 9, 0.0952 for the 20, about 0.00995 for the 200); each EMA(N) puts about 86.5% of its weight on its last N bars. **By convention:** the 9 shows immediate momentum, the 20 the short-to-medium trend and pullback zone, the 200 the long-term trend filter. **Evidence:** the 200-day average improved risk-adjusted returns on indices (CFA Institute, Faber), but a 41-market study found predictability faded and low costs erased profits (Rink 2023); an EMA-crossover walk-forward on crypto only matched buy-and-hold (break-even cost about 0.4% per trade). The long-horizon tests use the **SMA**, not the 200 EMA; that gap and the "institutional 200" claim are logged as open questions.
